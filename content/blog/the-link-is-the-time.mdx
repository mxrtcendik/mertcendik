title: The Link Is the Time

description: The URL is one UTC instant. Whoever opens it sees that moment in their own timezone.
---

[hourlink](https://hourlink.cendik.dev) has no account and no database. The meeting is the path.

`/2026-10-29T1100Z` is 11:00 UTC on that day. Whoever opens it sees that moment in their own timezone. The name, the length, and the cities are query parameters on the same link. Nothing is stored. Sending the link is the whole product.

I kept a UTC instant in the path because "Tuesday at 3pm" is not a moment until you say whose Tuesday. Two people are looking at one instant, written in two clocks. If the sender's city is on the link, the reader still sees their own time first, and the sender's time beside it. Other cities are the same instant again. When that instant falls on the next day there, the row says so. Eight cities is the limit.

The hour that happens twice, and the hour that does not happen, are the cases where a wall clock lies. When the clocks go back, the same numbers are two different moments, an earlier one and a later one. The page says that hour happens twice and lets you pick. When the clocks spring forward, a time in the gap is not a time. Choose a minute that the city skipped, and the page moves you to the next real time and says the one you typed does not exist that day. The path only ever contains one UTC instant. It does not contain a time that never occurred.

The length defaults to 30 minutes. The others are 15, 45, 60, and 90. A link with no length is the half hour.
